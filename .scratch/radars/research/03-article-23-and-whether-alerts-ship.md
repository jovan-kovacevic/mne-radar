# Ticket 03 — Does Montenegrin law restrict warning drivers about enforcement locations, and if it might, which product ships?

Researched 2026-09-06. Sources: primary statutory text where obtainable, marked as such.
Everything below is labelled VERIFIED (read in a primary or verbatim-quoting source),
INFERRED (my reading, not an authority's), or NOT ESTABLISHED.

---

## 1. Bottom line

- The article number in the ticket is **correct**. It is **Article 23** of the
  *Zakon o bezbjednosti saobraćaja na putevima* ("Sl. list CG", br. 33/12, 58/14,
  14/17 – odluka US, 66/19, **111/2026**). VERIFIED.
- Article 23 bans, on its face, a device or *means* by which the **operation** of
  speed-measuring devices "can be detected or interfered with". It contains **no**
  clause about warning of, or informing about, the **location** of enforcement
  equipment. VERIFIED (text below).
- France and Germany both had to **add an explicit clause** to reach location-warning
  software, because their base anti-detector wording did not. Montenegro has not added
  such a clause. That is the strongest structural argument that a static map is outside
  Article 23. INFERRED, but from verified comparative text.
- Whether a Montenegrin court or police officer would nonetheless read Article 23 onto a
  phone showing a static database is **NOT ESTABLISHED**. No Montenegrin case law,
  ministry guidance or commentary on this point was found. A German appellate court did
  exactly that stretch in 2015 under a *broader* wording, which is why this cannot be
  called safe.
- **The ticket's premise that "the exposure would land on users, not the publisher" is
  wrong.** Article 23 **stav 2** bans *putting into circulation or advertising* such
  devices/means, and Article 318 stav 1 tačka 2 fines the person who does it — the same
  500–1,000 EUR / up to 60 days band as the driver. If the app is a "sredstvo" under
  stav 1, the publisher is exposed too. VERIFIED (text below).
- **Publishing enforcement locations is not restricted.** No provision found. The
  Montenegrin Police Directorate itself publishes, in advance, where its interceptors
  and speed controls will be. VERIFIED.
- **Recommended shape: (b) — alerts default-off behind an informed acknowledgement**,
  plus naming/marketing constraints driven by Article 23 stav 2. This cannot be closed
  out without a Montenegrin lawyer (or a written MUP answer); see §9.

## 2. The operative provision, verbatim

Source: consolidated text at Paragraf Lex Montenegro
(https://www.paragraf.me/propisi-crnegore/zakon-o-bezbjednosti-saobracaja-na-putevima.html),
header line: `("Sl. list CG", br. 33/2012, 58/2014, 14/2017 - odluka US i 66/2019)`,
site currency stamp `OVAJ PROPIS JE AŽURAN NA DAN 31.3.2021. GODINE.`
Cross-checked against the original 2012 enactment PDF
(https://nbocg.me/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Zakon_o_bezbjednosti_saobracaja_na_putevima.pdf),
where the identical sentence appears and the penalty then sat in Article 319.

### Član 23 (the prohibition)

> **Član 23**
>
> U vozilu se u saobraćaju na putu ne smije koristiti niti nalaziti uređaj, odnosno
> sredstvo kojim se može otkrivati ili ometati rad uređaja za mjerenje brzine kretanja
> vozila, odnosno drugih uređaja namijenjenih za otkrivanje i dokumentovanje prekršaja.
>
> Zabranjeno je stavljati u promet ili reklamirati uređaje i sredstva iz stava 1 ovog
> člana.

Working translation (mine, not official):

> (1) In a vehicle in traffic on a road, there may not be used **nor be present** a
> device, or means, **by which the operation of** devices for measuring the speed of
> vehicles, or of other devices intended for the detection and documenting of offences,
> **can be detected or interfered with**.
> (2) It is prohibited to put into circulation or to advertise the devices and means
> referred to in paragraph 1 of this Article.

### Član 318 (the penalty)

> **Član 318**
>
> Novčanom kaznom u iznosu od 300 eura do 1.000 eura ili kaznom zatvora do 60 dana
> kazniće se za prekršaj:
>
> 1) vozač koji u vozilu u saobraćaju na putu koristi uređaj, odnosno sredstvo kojim se
> može otkrivati ili ometati rad uređaja za mjerenje brzine kretanja vozila, odnosno
> drugih uređaja namijenjenih za otkrivanje i dokumentovanje prekršaja **ili se takvi
> uređaji ili sredstva nađu u vozilu** (član 23 stav 1);
>
> 2) lice koje stavlja u promet ili reklamira uređaje i sredstva iz člana 23 stav 1 ovog
> zakona (član 23 stav 2);

The Government's 2025/26 amendment bill raises this band: `„Novčanom kaznom u iznosu od
500 eura do 1.000 eura ili kaznom zatvora do 60 dana kazniće se za prekršaj:"`
(Član 59 of the bill — https://wapi.gov.me/download/2de5e863-5722-4625-b724-92e48829d2b0?version=1.0).

### Član 296 (arrest power) — the part that actually matters for user risk

> **Član 296**
>
> Ovlašćeni policijski službenik će, u skladu sa zakonom kojim se uređuju prekršaji,
> **lišiti slobode** vozača, mladog vozača, vozača početnika ili instruktora vožnje:
> …
> 6) koji **posjeduje ili koristi** uređaj, odnosno sredstvo kojim se može otkrivati ili
> ometati rad uređaja za mjerenje brzine kretanja vozila, odnosno drugih uređaja
> namijenjenih za otkrivanje i dokumentovanje prekršaja;

This is a *shall*, not a *may*: an officer who forms the view that the thing in the car
is such a "sredstvo" is directed to deprive the driver of liberty. That is the single
most important fact in this ticket — the downside for a user is not a ticket, it is a
roadside arrest.

VERIFIED — all three articles read in the consolidated text; Art. 296 t. 6 and the
Art. 318 wording independently confirmed in the 2019 amendment bill
(https://wapi.gov.me/download/f1f9d8e7-2d5e-439a-af4b-c29b4947e2fe?version=1.0, Član 86
and Član 97).

## 4. The crucial distinction: static database vs active detection

### 4a. What the Montenegrin text actually says

Both verbs — *otkrivati* (detect) and *ometati* (interfere with) — govern the same
object: **`rad uređaja`**, "the **operation** of the devices". The prohibited thing is a
means by which *the operation of enforcement equipment* can be detected or jammed.

- A radar detector detects the *operation* — it senses emitted K/Ka-band or laser energy.
  Squarely inside.
- A jammer interferes with the *operation*. Squarely inside.
- A static, offline list of 88 published coordinates detects nothing. It does not know
  whether any device is switched on, present, or ever built. It recalls a coordinate from
  a government document. INFERRED: outside the plain words.

Two textual features cut the other way and must be stated honestly:

1. **`sredstvo`** ("means") is deliberately broader than `uređaj` ("device"). The
   legislator anticipated non-hardware.
2. **`kojim se MOŽE otkrivati`** is a *capability* test, not a *purpose* test. Montenegro
   has no equivalent of the German `dafür bestimmt ist` ("intended for") or the French
   `destiné à`. So "the app isn't *meant* as a detector" is not, on these words, an
   answer — the question is what it *can* do.
3. **Mere presence is the offence.** `ne smije koristiti niti nalaziti` — "may not be
   used **nor be present**". Art. 318 t.1 repeats it: `ili se takvi uređaji ili sredstva
   nađu u vozilu`. There is no "while in use" limb for the possession offence. A
   default-off toggle does not, by itself, take a phone out of the possession limb if the
   thing on it is a "sredstvo".

### 4b. France — the instructive contrast

Article R413-15, Code de la route (Légifrance,
https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000025111528/):

> **I.** « Le fait de détenir ou de transporter un appareil, dispositif ou produit de
> nature ou présenté comme étant de nature à **déceler la présence** ou **perturber le
> fonctionnement** d'appareils, instruments ou systèmes servant à la constatation des
> infractions à la législation ou à la réglementation de la circulation routière ou de
> permettre de se soustraire à la constatation desdites infractions est puni de l'amende
> prévue pour les contraventions de la cinquième classe. »
>
> **V.** « Les dispositions du présent article sont également applicables aux dispositifs
> ou produits **visant à avertir ou informer de la localisation** d'appareils,
> instruments ou systèmes servant à la constatation des infractions à la législation ou
> à la réglementation de la circulation routière. »

This is the whole argument in one place. France's paragraph I is *broader* than
Montenegro's Article 23 — it reaches detecting the **presence**, not merely the
operation — and France still judged it necessary to legislate paragraph V to reach
location-warning products. Montenegro has paragraph I's narrower cousin and nothing like
paragraph V.

What France then did with paragraph V is also relevant to product shape: rather than kill
the category, the state and the industry signed a protocol (28 July 2011) converting
*avertisseurs de radars* into *assistants d'aide à la conduite*, which may not signal a
camera's precise position but may signal a **"zone de danger"** — 4 km on motorway, 2 km
outside built-up areas, 500 m inside them — which may or may not contain a camera.
(Secondary sources: fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coyote_(système); legipermis.com. VERIFIED only
as "widely and consistently reported"; I did not read the protocol itself.)

### 4c. Germany — the cautionary contrast

§ 23 Abs. 1c StVO, verbatim (gesetze-im-internet.de,
https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__23.html):

> "Wer ein Fahrzeug führt, darf ein technisches Gerät nicht betreiben oder betriebsbereit
> mitführen, das dafür bestimmt ist, Verkehrsüberwachungsmaßnahmen **anzuzeigen** oder zu
> stören. Das gilt insbesondere für Geräte zur Störung oder Anzeige von
> Geschwindigkeitsmessungen (Radarwarn- oder Laserstörgeräte). Bei anderen technischen
> Geräten, die neben anderen Nutzungszwecken auch zur Anzeige oder Störung von
> Verkehrsüberwachungsmaßnahmen verwendet werden können, dürfen die entsprechenden
> Gerätefunktionen nicht verwendet werden."

Two things follow.

- The German verb is **`anzeigen`** — to *display/indicate* enforcement measures. That
  plainly covers a static database rendered on a screen. Montenegro's verb is
  `otkrivati … rad` — to *detect the operation*. The German wording is materially wider.
- Even so, Germany needed the **third sentence** (added by the 2020 StVO amendment) to
  put smartphone apps beyond argument — and *before* that sentence existed, **OLG Celle,
  03.11.2015, 2 Ss (OWi) 313/15** already held that carrying an operational smartphone
  with an activated "Blitzer-App" violates the provision. VERIFIED (decision indexed at
  dejure.org and NI-VORIS; NJW 2015, 3733).

The Celle decision is the reason this ticket cannot be closed as "obviously fine". A
court willing to reach the result will reach it. Note the honest counterweight: Celle was
construing `anzeigen`, a verb Montenegro does not use.

German practice has since gone further still: OLG Karlsruhe held the driver liable where
the app was running on a **passenger's** phone.

### 4d. Waze's compliance precedent

Waze staff announcement, 15 June 2013
(https://www.waze.com/discuss/t/removal-of-speed-cameras-following-legal-issues/146114):

> "We have just removed from the map all the speed cameras in Denmark, Switzerland and
> Germany due to it being illegal in these countries."

with a note that the team was "examining ways to make Waze compliant with French law as
well". VERIFIED as an official Waze staff post; the specific 2013 country list is dated
and I did **not** verify Waze's *current* per-country configuration — the current Waze
help page on camera alerts says nothing about country restrictions
(https://support.google.com/waze/answer/15758855).

The precedent worth taking is the *shape*, not the country list: the incumbent's answer
in a restrictive jurisdiction was to **remove the camera layer entirely** while keeping a
speed-limit-based over-speed warning, i.e. warn about the driver's behaviour rather than
about the enforcement.

NOT ESTABLISHED: whether Waze currently shows camera alerts in Montenegro. That would be
a useful empirical data point (a large operator's own legal read of Article 23) and is
cheap to check on a phone in-country. Recommend someone does.

## 5. Is publishing enforcement locations restricted?

**No provision found, and the state does it itself.**

- I grepped the full consolidated law for `tajn` (secret) and `povjerljiv`
  (confidential): **zero hits**. VERIFIED.
- Article 298 ("Snimanje saobraćaja i učesnika u saobraćaju korišćenjem odgovarajućih
  sredstava") authorises the state to record traffic and to use unmarked interceptor
  vehicles. It imposes no confidentiality on where enforcement happens, and no obligation
  to sign-post it. VERIFIED.
- The Police Directorate publishes its enforcement locations **in advance, by name**.
  Example, 27 July 2026: *"Mobilna jedinica policije za bezbjednost saobraćaja, tzv.
  „presretači", danas i sjutra, 27. i 28. jula biće angažovana na auto-putu Princeza
  Ksenija, kao i na magistralnim putnim pravcima Podgorica–Vilusi, Podgorica–Virpazar,
  Podgorica–Cetinje i Podgorica–Danilovgrad–Nikšić."*
  (https://www.portalanalitika.me/clanak/pojacanje-kontrole-na-putevima-evo-na-kojim-lokacijama-ce-danas-i-sjutra-biti-presretaci)
  VERIFIED via that report; the underlying announcement is the Uprava policije's own.
- The 88-location programme is already public in mainstream media: *"Stacionarni radari
  do kraja godine na 88 lokacija u Crnoj Gori"* (Boka News, 1 March 2026, citing RTCG,
  https://bokanews.me/stacionarni-radari-do-kraja-godine-na-88-lokacija-u-crnoj-gori/),
  naming route segments Podgorica–Cetinje (Donja Gorica, Šteke, Barutani, Meterizi,
  Obzovica), Podgorica–Nikšić, Virpazar, Bar, Ulcinj. The count matches the source PDF's
  88 Locations exactly.

**The real publication risk is not "publishing locations". It is Article 23 stav 2** —
`Zabranjeno je stavljati u promet ili reklamirati uređaje i sredstva iz stava 1`. If the
app is characterised as a stav 1 "sredstvo", then shipping it and advertising it is a
separate offence by the publisher, at 500–1,000 EUR or up to 60 days (Art. 318 t. 2).
This is a direct correction to the ticket's framing.

Practical consequence: **the app's name, store listing, and marketing copy are legal
surface, not just marketing.** Describing it as an "anti-radar", "radar detector", or
"radar warner" would be evidence for the prosecution on both stav 1 (what the means is
"for") and stav 2 (advertising it). Describing it as a map of officially published
enforcement locations with a proximity notice is both accurate and materially safer.

Side note for a different ticket, flagged not resolved: the same Boka News/RTCG report
states positions may be rotated so drivers cannot predict them. If true that is a data-
vintage problem, not a legal one. NOT VERIFIED — it is a news paraphrase and it sits oddly
with "stationary" installation.

## 7. Honest statement of the legal position

**Established:** the text of Article 23, its penalties (300–1,000 EUR now, 500–1,000 EUR
under the pending band, or up to 60 days), and the mandatory arrest power in Article
296 t. 6. Article 23 has not been amended since 2012.

**Not established:** whether Article 23 reaches a static, offline database of officially
published locations. The plain words point away from it — the object of the verb is `rad
uređaja`, the operation of the equipment, which a database does not touch — and the
France/Germany drafting history shows that legislatures which wanted to reach
location-warning products said so expressly. But no Montenegrin authority has said so,
`sredstvo` is broad, `može` is a capability test with no purpose element, mere presence in
the vehicle is the offence, and a German appellate court reached apps under a
comparable-but-wider clause before its legislature spelled it out.

**This is a genuine 60/40, not a 95/5. It should not be closed by an engineer.**

## 8. Recommended product shape

**(b) — alerts default-off behind an informed acknowledgement.** Concretely:

1. **Alerts ship, but off.** A first-run/first-Drive gate the user must pass deliberately.
   Not a checkbox in settings they never see; a screen they must act on.
2. **The acknowledgement states the law, not a euphemism.** Article 23 stav 1 verbatim in
   Montenegrin, an English translation, the penalty range from Article 318, and — most
   importantly — that Article 296 directs an officer to *deprive the driver of liberty*.
   A user who turns alerts on after reading that has made a real decision. A user who
   never sees it has had one made for them.
3. **No active detection of anything, ever.** No radio/optical sensing, no
   accelerometer-based "is that a camera" heuristics. This is what Article 23 is
   unambiguously about and there is no argument to be had.
4. **No live crowd-sourced reports and no mobile-patrol positions.** A user-reported
   "police here now" feed is functionally a distributed detector of enforcement
   *operation* — the exact object of `otkrivati … rad uređaja`. It is also the thing that
   would move the app from "map of a published government document" to "warning network",
   which is the characterisation to avoid. Keep it off the roadmap, not just out of v1.
5. **Naming and store copy are legal surface (Art. 23 st. 2).** Never "anti-radar",
   "radar detector", "radar warner", "antiradar". Position as: a map of the enforcement
   locations published in the Government's own document, with an optional proximity
   notice. Cite the source document prominently — the map's standing decision that
   citing the source is the best trust answer is *also* the best legal-characterisation
   answer.
6. **Consider the France-style softening as a fallback, not a default.** If counsel comes
   back cautious rather than negative, "you are entering a section with enforcement
   locations" over a zone is a smaller target than "camera in 400 m". Do not build it
   speculatively; it is a copy-and-radius change, cheap to make later.
7. **Do not ship (a).** Map-only does not actually de-risk much — a map of camera
   positions in a vehicle is subject to the same `sredstvo` argument — and it destroys the
   product. If counsel says the provision reaches the app at all, the honest answer is not
   to launch publicly in Montenegro, not to launch a defanged version and hope.

**Blocking item for public launch:** one written opinion from a Montenegrin advokat on
whether Article 23 stav 1 reaches a static database app, and whether stav 2 exposes the
publisher. Run a free-access-to-information request to MUP in parallel. Until one of those
comes back, (b) is the shape that ships; if either comes back negative, the alert feature
does not ship in Montenegro at all.
